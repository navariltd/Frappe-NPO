# 3. Donations

The donation feature answers three questions: who gave, how much of it has actually
arrived, and what was it spent on. Almost everything else in this chapter exists to
keep those three answers honest.

## 3.1 What you can do

- Keep a donor register, grouped by donor type (individual, corporate, trust, foundation,
  religious body …) so you can see giving by segment.
- Record a **pledge** and then each payment against it as the money arrives — so
  "pledged" and "received" are never confused.
- Record a one-off gift in a single step when no instalments are involved.
- Issue an **80G tax-exemption certificate** for a donation, numbered and printable.
- **Allocate** each donation to the cause it was given for: a beneficiary, a student, a
  learning centre, a budget, a project, an employee, or general/admin.
- Collect donations into **funding pools** and spend from the pool rather than from a
  single gift — useful for matched funding and campaign buckets.
- Accept donations online through Razorpay, with the receipt created automatically.
- Report on donations, on the payments behind them, on allocation, and on which budgets
  were funded by donations.

## 3.2 Pledges versus money received

A **Donation** is a commitment. The **payment table** on it records what has actually
landed. That split matters:

- A supporter promises 50,000 in four instalments — one donation, four payments.
- The list shows what was pledged and what is still unpaid, so your fund-raising total
  and your bank balance can both be right.
- When a payment is recorded, the app can also post the accounting entry (Payment Entry)
  for you — on, by default; a finance team that prefers to check each one can switch
  that off in settings.
- From a submitted donation you can create the Payment Entry directly, and from a
  Payment Entry that references a donation you can jump straight to allocation.

If you never track pledges, switch the payment table off in settings and a donation is
just money in the bank.

## 3.3 Allocating a donation — saying where it went

Allocation is what turns "we received 5 lakh" into "we received 5 lakh and 3 lakh fed
42 families in Ward 7".

- Create an allocation against the donation and pick the recipient: a beneficiary, a
  student, a learning centre, a budget, a project, an employee, or general/admin.
- You can allocate in several rows — one donation can fund several causes, and one cause
  can draw on several donations.
- **The app will not let you allocate more than the donation has actually received.**
  Pledges that have not arrived cannot be spent; that single rule prevents most
  over-spending mistakes.
- Allocation rows point at the source payment entry where relevant, so every rupee in a
  programme report traces back to a donor and a bank transaction.
- The allocation form also offers a **Budget** shortcut: pick a budget and the app fills
  in what remains available, so you allocate against real headroom.

## 3.4 Funding pools

A funding pool is a bucket of money for a purpose — "Corpus", "Emergency relief 2026",
"Education matched fund". Donations are allocated into the pool; disbursements and
programme spend draw from it. Use pools when money arrives unrestricted but must be
reported against a theme, and skip them for small shops where every donation is spent
on a named cause.

## 3.5 80G certificates

For donors who need a tax receipt:

1. The company's **80G number and PAN** must be filled in settings first — the
   certificate cannot be issued without them, and the app will stop you rather than
   print a defective receipt.
2. Create the certificate from the donation; it is numbered automatically and prints
   with the donation details.
3. **One certificate per donation.** If a donor needs a consolidated annual receipt,
   issue one per donation and hand them together — the app deliberately does not
   produce duplicates for the same gift.
4. Amend or cancel rather than edit history if a certificate was wrong.

## 3.6 The five donation reports

| Report                         | The question it answers                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Donation**                   | What came in during a period, pledged vs received vs distributed.            |
| **Donation Payments**          | Which individual payments make up that total — reconcile this with the bank. |
| **Donor Contribution**         | Where each donor's money actually went, by recipient and project.            |
| **Donation Allocation**        | What is allocated, to whom, and how much is still sitting unallocated.       |
| **Donation Budget Allocation** | How much of each budget was funded by donations.                             |

Start with **Donation Allocations** at month end: any donation with a balance still
unallocated is either unrestricted money you have not yet programmed, or a gap in your
record keeping. Both are worth knowing before a board meeting.
