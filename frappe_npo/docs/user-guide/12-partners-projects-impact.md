# 12. Partners, recruitment, projects and impact

This chapter is the "who we work with and what changed" side: partner organisations,
intake rounds, and the numbers you report to donors and boards.

## 12.1 Organisations and partners

Two registers, deliberately kept apart:

- **Organisation** — the agencies you deliver through or alongside: partner NGOs, trusts,
  societies, government bodies, companies. Organisations are what care centres, learning
  centres and user profiles belong to. Mark one _Inactive_ when a partnership ends rather
  than deleting it — history refers to it.
- **Partner** — the individuals and institutions that bring you beneficiaries, funds or
  services: a community leader, a school, a corporate CSR partner, a referral agency.
  A partner can also be a customer or a supplier, so the same relationship can be invoiced
  or paid.

Partners attach to beneficiaries and to impact records, which is what makes "what did
partner X deliver this year?" answerable.

## 12.2 Recruitment phases — controlled intake

A **Recruitment Phase** is an intake round: a survey, a camp, a referral drive, a
funded cohort.

- Give the round a title and a period, and say how many people it can take.
- **The app counts the slots for you.** Available slots are recalculated from the number
  of _Active_ beneficiaries tagged to that round; if more people are tagged than there are
  slots, saving is blocked with the current counts. That single control is what stops a
  funded cohort of 100 quietly becoming 140.
- Split a round across branches when several teams share the intake.
- Tie the round to a **funding pool** so you always know which money paid for it.
- Give each round its own beneficiary number series, so IDs from different intakes never
  look alike.

Tagging a beneficiary to their recruitment phase is what makes cohort reporting possible
later: of the 120 people recruited in this round, how many are active, how many left, and
why.

## 12.3 Projects, cost centres and budgets

The financial scaffolding is standard accounting, extended with beneficiary awareness:

- **Project** — a time-bounded piece of work: a round, a campaign, a shelter programme.
  Donations, disbursements, payment entries and stock entries all carry a project, so
  cost per project is a given rather than a spreadsheet exercise.
- **Cost centre / programme** — which department or programme bears the cost; beneficiaries
  carry a programme, which becomes the accounting dimension.
- **Budget** — what you may spend. Donations can be allocated into a budget, and the
  donation budget report shows how much of each budget came from donors.
- **Project level** — a simple grading (state, district, community) so large programmes can
  be rolled up.
- **Agent projects** — with the setting on, every field agent gets a project from a
  template, so agent-level cost is visible without extra bookkeeping.

Set the dimensions up before you post anything; they cannot be added to entries made
earlier.

## 12.4 Impact measurement

The record that sits behind a funder report: what a partner delivered, for how many
people, at what cost, against which goals.

- Create one record per partner per period.
- Enter the money spent and the number of people reached; **cost per beneficiary is
  calculated for you** — usually the first number a funder asks for.
- Note whether the beneficiary was an individual, a school or a group, and name the
  school or group.
- Tick the outcomes that actually apply — increased enrolment, academic improvement,
  retention, wellbeing, access to equal opportunities. Leave the rest unticked; a form
  where everything is ticked convinces nobody.
- Attach the **SDGs** the work contributes to, one per line, and use **key result areas**
  (with weightages) where the funder wants a score.

The masters behind it — SDG, key result area, strategic focus, thematic area — are there
so that many projects can be reported in the same vocabulary. Set them up once, with the
programme team, not per report.
