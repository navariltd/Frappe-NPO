# 8. Cases

A **case** is a need: someone needs shelter, food, a legal document, medical treatment,
an entitlement, a family meeting. Cases are how field work is assigned, followed up and
closed — and how you prove the work happened.

## 8.1 What the app does for you

- Files each need against a beneficiary, with a type, a priority and the story in the
  person's own words.
- Assigns the work: to a person or a team, with a separate note of who is handling the
  legal side when a paralegal is involved.
- Tracks follow-up: each contact, visit and outcome is dated, so "what happened last
  time?" is never guesswork.
- Records updates service by service — shelter, medical, food, entitlement, legal, family
  meeting — each with its outcome, so the case reads like a case file rather than a diary.
- Closes with a reason, and can update the person's status or living situation as part of
  closing, so nobody re-types the same information.
- Gives every worker a **My Cases** worklist, and managers the weekly charts.

## 8.2 Working a case

1. **Open it** from the beneficiary (or from the case list), choose the type and priority.
2. **Assign it.** A case without an owner does not get worked.
3. **Follow up as it happens.** Logging at the end of the week loses the detail that makes
   the record useful in a review.
4. **Record the outcome** in the matching update. This is the difference between "we
   visited" and "it was resolved": *Hospitalised*, *Treatment done*, *Referred to other
   organisation*, *Complaint registered*, *Referred to agency*, *Resolved and reported*,
   *Meeting went well and the beneficiary returned home*, and so on.
5. **Close it** with the right closure reason, and let closing update the person's status.

## 8.3 Statuses and priorities

- **Status:** *New* → *In Follow Up* → *Closed*. *Untraced* when the person cannot be
  reached; *Spam* for duplicates and bogus entries fielded by a helpline.
- **Priority:** *Urgent*, *High*, *Medium*, *Low*. Use it honestly — if everything is
  urgent, nothing is.

Do not invent extra statuses. If the work needs more nuance, the follow-up and update
sections carry it.

## 8.4 What managers watch

- **My Cases** — each worker's own worklist, filtered by status.
- **Cases per week** chart — throughput. A flat line with a rising open count means the
  team is not closing.
- **Closed Cases** number card — the figure you quote to a board.
- **Rescues per week** and **Entitlement Requests per week** — the other two streams of
  field activity, which often explain a case spike.

## 8.5 Habits that keep case data usable

- **One case per need.** Bundling medical, shelter and legal asks into one case means you
  cannot report on them, close them separately, or hand them to different teams.
- **Follow-ups with dates** beat a paragraph in a description.
- **Close on the person's terms**, when the problem is solved — not when the funding
  period ends.
- **Cases are how services get delivered.** Shelter admission, hospitalisation, food
  support, entitlement requests and legal referrals all start as a case and record their
  outcomes there (see [chapter 10](10-services.md)).
